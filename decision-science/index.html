Decision Science

The Science of Decision-Making

Decision Science examines how people evaluate options, handle uncertainty, predict outcomes, and turn intentions into action. Explore evidence-based articles about the brain, emotion, cognitive biases, probability, risk, habits, social influence, and practical ways to think more clearly about choices.

Where possible, we will link readers to original scientific papers, universities, research institutions, and other authoritative sources. Our aim is to encourage curiosity and critical thinking so readers can examine the evidence and reach their own conclusions. No research, statistics, quotations, authors, studies, or citations will be invented.

Start with the problem

What are you struggling with?

I can’t stop thinking about a decision. One more comparison rarely creates certainty. I have too many options. More choice can create more work without creating a better answer. I keep postponing something important. Avoidance may be protecting you from an uncomfortable feeling. I’m exhausted from making choices. Mental load can make the easiest option feel more attractive than the careful one. I’m afraid I made the wrong decision. A bad outcome does not automatically prove the decision was bad. Everything takes longer than I expect. Plans often focus on the ideal path and neglect ordinary delays. I’m not sure how risky something really is. Fear, familiarity, and control can distort how danger feels. I’m stuck because I’ve already invested too much. Past costs cannot be recovered by spending more.
